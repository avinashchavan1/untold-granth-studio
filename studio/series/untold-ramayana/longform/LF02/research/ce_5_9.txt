5.9.1
  अवधूय च तां बुद्धिं बभूवावस्थितस्तदा ।
  जगाम चापरां चिन्तां सीतां प्रति महाकपिः ॥
  avadhūya ca tāṃ buddhiṃ babhūvāvasthitastadā, jagāma cāparāṃ cintāṃ sītāṃ prati mahākapiḥ
  And having discarded that thought, he became calm. The great monkey (Hanuman) then turned his attention to another thought concerning Sītā.

5.9.2
  न रामेण वियुक्ता सा स्वप्तुमर्हति भामिनी ।
  न भोक्तुं नाप्यलंकर्तुं न पानमुपसेवितुम् ॥
  na rāmeṇa viyuktā sā svaptumarhati bhāminī, na bhoktuṃ nāpyalaṃkartuṃ na pānamupasevitum
  That splendid lady (Sītā), separated from Rāma, does not deserve to sleep, nor to eat, nor to adorn herself, nor to partake in beverages.

5.9.3
  न हि रामसमः कश्चिद्विद्यते त्रिदशेष्वपि ।
  अन्येयमिति निश्चित्य पानभूमौ चचार सः ॥
  
  Having determined that 'this is not she,' he (Rāvaṇa) wandered in the drinking hall. For (Sītā) would not approach any other man, nor even the lord of the gods, since indeed, no one among the immortals is equal to Rāma.

5.9.4
  क्रीडितेनापराः क्लान्ता गीतेन च तथा पराः ।
  नृत्तेन चापराः क्लान्ताः पानविप्रहतास्तथा ॥
  krīḍitenāparāḥ klāntā gītena ca tathā parāḥ, nṛttena cāparāḥ klāntāḥ pānaviprahatāstathā
  Some women were tired from playing, and similarly others from singing. Still others were exhausted from dancing, and similarly, some were overcome by drink.

5.9.5
  मुरजेषु मृदङ्गेषु पीठिकासु च संस्थिताः ।
  तथास्तरणमुख्य्येषु संविष्टाश्चापराः स्त्रियः ॥
  murajeṣu mṛdaṅgeṣu pīṭhikāsu ca saṃsthitāḥ, tathāstaraṇamukhyyeṣu saṃviṣṭāścāparāḥ striyaḥ
  Some women were resting on drums (murajas), clay drums (mṛdaṅgas), and on low seats. Similarly, other women were seated on excellent couches.

5.9.6
  अङ्गनानां सहस्रेण भूषितेन विभूषणैः ।
  रूपसंलापशीलेन युक्तगीतार्थभाषिणा ॥
  aṅganānāṃ sahasreṇa bhūṣitena vibhūṣaṇaiḥ, rūpasaṃlāpaśīlena yuktagītārthabhāṣiṇā
  The drinking hall (was furnished) with a thousand women, adorned with ornaments, whose nature was to engage in charming conversations, and who spoke words appropriate to their songs.

5.9.7
  देशकालाभियुक्तेन युक्तवाक्याभिधायिना ।
  रताभिरतसंसुप्तं ददर्श हरियूथपः ॥
  deśakālābhiyuktena yuktavākyābhidhāyinā, ratābhiratasaṃsuptaṃ dadarśa hariyūthapaḥ
  The leader of the monkeys (Hanuman), acting suitably according to the circumstances of time and place and speaking appropriate words [to himself], observed [Ravana] deeply asleep, immersed in sensual pleasures.

5.9.8
  तासां मध्ये महाबाहुः शुशुभे राक्षसेश्वरः ।
  गोष्ठे महति मुख्यानां गवां मध्ये यथा वृषः ॥
  tāsāṃ madhye mahābāhuḥ śuśubhe rākṣaseśvaraḥ, goṣṭhe mahati mukhyānāṃ gavāṃ madhye yathā vṛṣaḥ
  Among them, the mighty-armed lord of demons shone, just like a bull among the principal cows in a large cattle pen.

5.9.9
  स राक्षसेन्द्रः शुशुभे ताभिः परिवृतः स्वयम् ।
  करेणुभिर्यथारण्यं परिकीर्णो महाद्विपः ॥
  sa rākṣasendraḥ śuśubhe tābhiḥ parivṛtaḥ svayam, kareṇubhiryathāraṇyaṃ parikīrṇo mahādvipaḥ
  That lord of demons himself shone, surrounded by those women, just as a great elephant, encircled by female elephants, shines beautifully in a forest.

5.9.10
  सर्वकामैरुपेतां च पानभूमिं महात्मनः ।
  ददर्श कपिशार्दूलस्तस्य रक्षःपतेर्गृहे ॥
  sarvakāmairupetāṃ ca pānabhūmiṃ mahātmanaḥ, dadarśa kapiśārdūlastasya rakṣaḥpatergṛhe
  The best of monkeys (Hanuman) observed in the house of that great-souled lord of rākṣasas a drinking place equipped with all desired amenities.

5.9.11
  मृगाणां महिषाणां च वराहाणां च भागशः ।
  तत्र न्यस्तानि मांसानि पानभूमौ ददर्श सः ॥
  mṛgāṇāṃ mahiṣāṇāṃ ca varāhāṇāṃ ca bhāgaśaḥ, tatra nyastāni māṃsāni pānabhūmau dadarśa saḥ
  He saw there, in the drinking hall, portions of meat from deer, buffaloes, and boars, laid out.

5.9.12
  रौक्मेषु च विशलेषु भाजनेष्वर्धभक्षितान् ।
  ददर्श कपिशार्दूल मयूरान् कुक्कुटांस्तथा ॥
  raukmeṣu ca viśaleṣu bhājaneṣvardhabhakṣitān, dadarśa kapiśārdūla mayūrān kukkuṭāṃstathā
  (saḥ) raukmeṣu ca viśāleṣu bhājaneṣu

5.9.13
  वराहवार्ध्राणसकान्दधिसौवर्चलायुतान् ।
  शल्यान्मृगमयूरांश्च हनूमानन्ववैक्षत ॥
  varāhavārdhrāṇasakāndadhisauvarcalāyutān, śalyānmṛgamayūrāṃśca hanūmānanvavaikṣata
  Hanuman closely observed the porcupines and the deer and peacocks, as well as the boar and vārdhrāṇasaka meats prepared with curd and black salt.

5.9.14
  महिषानेकशल्यांश्च छागांश्च कृतनिष्ठितान् ।
  लेख्यमुच्चावचं पेयं भोज्यानि विविधानि च ॥
  
  He saw various kinds of well-prepared partridges, partially eaten chakoras, buffaloes prepared with a single porcupine, and well-cooked goats, along with various written documents, drinks, and many kinds of food items.

5.9.15
  तथाम्ललवणोत्तंसैर्विविधै रागषाडवैः ।
  हार नूपुरकेयूरैरपविद्धैर्महाधनैः ॥
  tathāmlalavaṇottaṃsairvividhai rāgaṣāḍavaiḥ, hāra nūpurakeyūrairapaviddhairmahādhanaiḥ
  Similarly, with various sour and salty appetizers, relishes, and six-flavored dishes, and with very valuable necklaces, anklets, and armlets scattered around.

5.9.16
  पानभाजनविक्षिप्तैः फलैश्च विविधैरपि ।
  कृतपुष्पोपहारा भूरधिकं पुष्यति श्रियम् ॥
  pānabhājanavikṣiptaiḥ phalaiśca vividhairapi, kṛtapuṣpopahārā bhūradhikaṃ puṣyati śriyam
  With various fruits and scattered drinking vessels, the earth (bhū), adorned with flower offerings, greatly enhances its beauty.

5.9.17
  तत्र तत्र च विन्यस्तैः सुश्लिष्टैः शयनासनैः ।
  पानभूमिर्विना वह्निं प्रदीप्तेवोपलक्ष्यते ॥
  tatra tatra ca vinyastaiḥ suśliṣṭaiḥ śayanāsanaiḥ, pānabhūmirvinā vahniṃ pradīptevopalakṣyate
  And with comfortably arranged beds and seats placed here and there, the drinking hall appears as if brightly lit, even without a fire.

5.9.18
  बहुप्रकारैर्विविधैर्वरसंस्कारसंस्कृतैः ।
  मांसैः कुशलसंयुक्तैः पानभूमिगतैः पृथक् ॥
  bahuprakārairvividhairvarasaṃskārasaṃskṛtaiḥ, māṃsaiḥ kuśalasaṃyuktaiḥ pānabhūmigataiḥ pṛthak
  With many kinds of various meats, excellently seasoned and prepared, skillfully combined, and laid out separately in the drinking hall.

5.9.19
  शर्करासवमाध्वीकाः पुष्पासवफलासवाः ।
  वासचूर्णैश्च विविधैर्मृष्टास्तैस्तैः पृथक्पृथक् ॥
  
  Divine, clear, and various kinds of liquor, even those prepared by celestials, along with sugarcane-juice-based and honey-based wines, and those made from flower and fruit juices, were separately mixed with various scented powders.

5.9.20
  हिरण्मयैश्च करकैर्भाजनैः स्फाटिकैरपि ।
  जाम्बूनदमयैश्चान्यैः करकैरभिसंवृता ॥
  
  The ground, covered with numerous garlands, shone beautifully. It was also encompassed by golden pitchers, crystal vessels, and other pitchers made of Jambūnada gold.

5.9.21
  राजतेषु च कुम्भेषु जाम्बूनदमयेषु च ।
  पानश्रेष्ठं तदा भूरि कपिस्तत्र ददर्श ह ॥
  rājateṣu ca kumbheṣu jāmbūnadamayeṣu ca, pānaśreṣṭhaṃ tadā bhūri kapistatra dadarśa ha
  There, the monkey (Hanuman) then saw a great quantity of the finest drinks in silver pitchers and also in those made of Jambūnada gold.

5.9.22
  सो ऽपश्यच्छातकुम्भानि शीधोर्मणिमयानि च ।
  राजतानि च पूर्णानि भाजनानि महाकपिः ॥
  so'paśyacchātakumbhāni śīdhormaṇimayāni ca, rājatāni ca pūrṇāni bhājanāni mahākapiḥ
  That great monkey (Hanuman) saw golden vessels filled with liquor, and also jewel-studded ones, as well as silver vessels, all full.

5.9.23
  क्व चिदर्धावशेषाणि क्व चित् पीतानि सर्वशः ।
  क्व चिन्नैव प्रपीतानि पानानि स ददर्श ह ॥
  kva cidardhāvaśeṣāṇi kva cit pītāni sarvaśaḥ, kva cinnaiva prapītāni pānāni sa dadarśa ha
  He saw drinks: some half-finished, some entirely consumed, and some not at all tasted.

5.9.24
  क्व चिद्भक्ष्यांश्च विविधान् क्व चित् पानानि भागशः ।
  क्व चिदन्नावशेषाणि पश्यन् वै विचचार ह ॥
  kva cidbhakṣyāṃśca vividhān kva cit pānāni bhāgaśaḥ, kva cidannāvaśeṣāṇi paśyan vai vicacāra ha
  Seeing various kinds of food, some partially eaten, drinks partially consumed, and some remaining food, he wandered around.

5.9.25
  क्व चित् प्रभिन्नैः करकैः क्व चिदालोडितैर्घटैः ।
  क्व चित् संपृक्तमाल्यानि जलानि च फलानि च ॥
  kva cit prabhinnaiḥ karakaiḥ kva cidāloḍitairghaṭaiḥ, kva cit saṃpṛktamālyāni jalāni ca phalāni ca
  Sometimes there were broken pitchers, sometimes stirred-up pots, and sometimes waters and fruits mixed with garlands.

5.9.26
  शयनान्यत्र नारीणां शून्यानि बहुधा पुनः ।
  परस्परं समाश्लिष्य काश्चित् सुप्ता वराङ्गनाः ॥
  śayanānyatra nārīṇāṃ śūnyāni bahudhā punaḥ, parasparaṃ samāśliṣya kāścit suptā varāṅganāḥ
  Here, the women's beds were often empty; however, some beautiful women lay sleeping, embracing each other.

5.9.27
  का चिच्च वस्त्रमन्यस्या अपहृत्योपगुह्य च ।
  उपगम्याबला सुप्ता निद्राबलपराजिता ॥
  kā cicca vastramanyasyā apahṛtyopaguhya ca, upagamyābalā suptā nidrābalaparājitā
  And one weak woman, overcome by the power of sleep, while asleep, drew near, took away another's garment, and embraced it.

5.9.28
  तासामुच्छ्वासवातेन वस्त्रं माल्यं च गात्रजम् ।
  नात्यर्थं स्पन्दते चित्रं प्राप्य मन्दमिवानिलम् ॥
  tāsāmucchvāsavātena vastraṃ mālyaṃ ca gātrajam, nātyarthaṃ spandate citraṃ prāpya mandamivānilam
  The garments and garlands worn on the bodies of those women do not flutter much from the breeze of their breath. It is remarkable how they are hardly stirred, as if by a gentle wind.

5.9.29
  चन्दनस्य च शीतस्य शीधोर्मधुरसस्य च ।
  विविधस्य च माल्यस्य पुष्पस्य विविधस्य च ॥
  candanasya ca śītasya śīdhormadhurasasya ca, vividhasya ca mālyasya puṣpasya vividhasya ca
  And of cool sandalwood, and of sweet liquor, and of various garlands, and of various flowers.

5.9.30
  स्नानानां चन्दनानां च धूपानां चैव मूर्छितः ।
  प्रववौ सुरभिर्गन्धो विमाने पुष्पके तदा ॥
  
  There, the wind, carrying various fragrances in many ways, became saturated with the aromas of bathings, sandal pastes, and incenses. Then, a fragrant scent wafted throughout the Puṣpaka aerial chariot.

5.9.31
  श्यामावदातास्तत्रान्याः काश्चित् कृष्णा वराङ्गनाः ।
  काश्चित् काञ्चनवर्णाङ्ग्यः प्रमदा राक्षसालये ॥
  śyāmāvadātāstatrānyāḥ kāścit kṛṣṇā varāṅganāḥ, kāścit kāñcanavarṇāṅgyaḥ pramadā rākṣasālaye
  In that demon's palace, some of the beautiful women were of dusky and fair complexions, others were dark-skinned, and some enchanting ladies had golden complexions.

5.9.32
  तासां निद्रावशत्वाच्च मदनेन विमूर्छितम् ।
  पद्मिनीनां प्रसुप्तानां रूपमासीद् यथैव हि ॥
  tāsāṃ nidrāvaśatvācca madanena vimūrchitam, padminīnāṃ prasuptānāṃ rūpamāsīd yathaiva hi
  Their appearance, subdued by sleep and swooning with passion, was indeed just like that of sleeping lotus flowers.

5.9.33
  एवं सर्वमशेषेण रावणान्तःपुरं कपिः ।
  ददर्श सुमहातेजा न ददर्श च जानकीम् ॥
  evaṃ sarvamaśeṣeṇa rāvaṇāntaḥpuraṃ kapiḥ, dadarśa sumahātejā na dadarśa ca jānakīm
  Thus, the greatly radiant monkey (kapi) completely saw all of Rāvaṇa's inner palace, but he did not see Jānakī.

5.9.34
  निरीक्षमाणश्च ततस्ताः स्त्रियः स महाकपिः ।
  जगाम महतीं चिन्तां धर्मसाध्वसशङ्कितः ॥
  nirīkṣamāṇaśca tatastāḥ striyaḥ sa mahākapiḥ, jagāma mahatīṃ cintāṃ dharmasādhvasaśaṅkitaḥ
  And then, that great monkey, observing those women, became greatly worried, apprehensive about the infringement of natural law (dharma).

5.9.35
  परदारावरोधस्य प्रसुप्तस्य निरीक्षणम् ।
  इदं खलु ममात्यर्थं धर्मलोपं करिष्यति ॥
  paradārāvarodhasya prasuptasya nirīkṣaṇam, idaṃ khalu mamātyarthaṃ dharmalopaṃ kariṣyati
  Observing the sleeping women within another's private chambers will indeed constitute a great transgression of my intrinsic duty (dharma).

5.9.36
  न हि मे परदाराणां दृष्टिर्विषयवर्तिनी ।
  अयं चात्र मया दृष्टः परदारपरिग्रहः ॥
  na hi me paradārāṇāṃ dṛṣṭirviṣayavartinī, ayaṃ cātra mayā dṛṣṭaḥ paradāraparigrahaḥ
  Indeed, my gaze does not naturally fall upon other men's wives. Yet, here, the seizure of another's wife has been observed by me.

5.9.37
  तस्य प्रादुरभूच्चिन्तापुनरन्या मनस्विनः ।
  निश्चितैकान्तचित्तस्य कार्यनिश्चयदर्शिनी ॥
  tasya prādurabhūccintāpunaranyā manasvinaḥ, niścitaikāntacittasya kāryaniścayadarśinī
  Another thought then arose for that noble-minded (manasvin) individual, whose mind was fixed and resolute, a thought which revealed the proper course of action.

5.9.38
  कामं दृष्ट्वा मया सर्वा विश्वस्ता रावणस्त्रियः ।
  न तु मे मनसः किं चिद्वैकृत्यमुपपद्यते ॥
  kāmaṃ dṛṣṭvā mayā sarvā viśvastā rāvaṇastriyaḥ, na tu me manasaḥ kiṃ cidvaikṛtyamupapadyate
  Though I have indeed seen all of Ravana's unsuspecting women, yet no mental perturbation whatsoever has arisen in my mind.

5.9.39
  मनो हि हेतुः सर्वेषामिन्द्रियाणां प्रवर्तते ।
  शुभाशुभास्ववस्थासु तच्च मे सुव्यवस्थितम् ॥
  mano hi hetuḥ sarveṣāmindriyāṇāṃ pravartate, śubhāśubhāsvavasthāsu tacca me suvyavasthitam
  Indeed, the mind is the cause that directs all the senses in both auspicious and inauspicious circumstances, and that very mind (manas) of mine is well-controlled.

5.9.40
  नान्यत्र हि मया शक्या वैदेही परिमार्गितुम् ।
  स्त्रियो हि स्त्रीषु दृश्यन्ते सदा संपरिमार्गणे ॥
  nānyatra hi mayā śakyā vaidehī parimārgitum, striyo hi strīṣu dṛśyante sadā saṃparimārgaṇe
  Indeed, Vaidehī (Sītā) cannot be searched for by me anywhere else, because women are always found among women when one is searching.

5.9.41
  यस्य सत्त्वस्य या योनिस्तस्यां तत् परिमार्ग्यते ।
  न शक्यं प्रमदा नष्टा मृगीषु परिमार्गितुम् ॥
  yasya sattvasya yā yonistasyāṃ tat parimārgyate, na śakyaṃ pramadā naṣṭā mṛgīṣu parimārgitum
  Whatever creature (sattva) has a certain species (yoni), it is searched for within that species. It is not possible to search for a lost woman among female deer.

5.9.42
  तदिदं मार्गितं तावच्छुद्धेन मनसा मया ।
  रावणान्तःपुरं सरं दृश्यते न च जानकी ॥
  tadidaṃ mārgitaṃ tāvacchuddhena manasā mayā, rāvaṇāntaḥpuraṃ saraṃ dṛśyate na ca jānakī
  Indeed, this much has been searched by me with a pure mind: the entire inner palace of Rāvaṇa has been seen, but Jānakī (Sītā) is not (seen).

5.9.43
  देवगन्धर्वकन्याश्च नागकन्याश्च वीर्यवान् ।
  अवेक्षमाणो हनुमान्नैवापश्यत जानकीम् ॥
  devagandharvakanyāśca nāgakanyāśca vīryavān, avekṣamāṇo hanumānnaivāpaśyata jānakīm
  The mighty Hanumān, though observing the daughters of the devas (celestial beings), gandharvas, and nāgas, could not find Jānakī at all.

5.9.44
  तामपश्यन् कपिस्तत्र पश्यंश्चान्या वरस्त्रियः ।
  अपक्रम्य तदा वीरः प्रध्यातुमुपचक्रमे ॥
  tāmapaśyan kapistatra paśyaṃścānyā varastriyaḥ, apakramya tadā vīraḥ pradhyātumupacakrame
  Not finding her (Sītā) there, even while seeing other excellent women, the heroic monkey (Hanumān) then withdrew and began to ponder deeply.
