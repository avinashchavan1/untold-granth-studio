# A89 (baby Hanuman and the sun) and A38 (forgot his powers): research notes

Source: Critical Edition text and translation at enjoylearningsanskrit.com (book-4/chapter-65, book-7/chapters-35, 36).

## Kishkindha 4.65 (Jambavan tells Hanuman his birth, to rouse him before the ocean leap)
- 4.65.8: Puñjikastalā, foremost apsaras, became Añjanā, wife of the vanara Keśarin.
- 4.65.14-18: Vayu (Maruta) embraces her "mentally"; promises a powerful, intelligent son.
- 4.65.19: as a child, seeing the rising sun in the forest, he wanted to seize it as a fruit and leapt into the sky.
- 4.65.20: after 300 yojanas, driven back by its radiance, he did not become dejected.
- 4.65.21-22: Indra, enraged, hurled the thunderbolt; on a mountain peak his LEFT jaw was broken: hence the name Hanuman.
- 4.65.23-24: Vayu, enraged, stopped blowing in the three worlds; the gods appeased him.
- 4.65.25-27: Brahma's boon: invulnerable to weapons in battle; Indra's boon: death only when he chooses.

## Uttara 7.35-36 (Agastya tells Rama)
- 7.35.16: a curse by sages: his strength was not known to him.
- 7.35.23-24: he saw the rising sun like a cluster of hibiscus flowers, and flew up wanting the fruit.
- 7.35.28-30: Vayu followed, keeping him cool; he travelled many thousands of yojanas and came near the sun; the sun did not burn him ("this child is innocent").
- 7.35.31-35: that same day Rahu came to seize the sun (eclipse); Hanuman met Rahu on the sun's chariot; Rahu complained to Indra that "another" had seized the sun.
- 7.35.39-44: Hanuman left the sun and chased Rahu; then saw Airavata and ran at it thinking "this too is a great fruit".
- 7.35.46-47: Indra struck him with the thunderbolt; he fell on the mountain and his LEFT jaw broke.
- 7.36.11: Indra: because his jaw (hanu) was injured, he will be known as Hanuman; 7.36.12 immune to the thunderbolt; 7.36.19 Brahma: immune to brahmin curses, long-lived.
- 7.36.32-33 (A38): Bhrigu and Angiras sages, harassed by the playful child, curse him: he will not know his own strength for a long time (until reminded).

## The popular version
- Hanuman Chalisa: "जुग सहस्र जोजन पर भानू। लील्यो ताहि मधुर फल जानू॥" (he swallowed the sun, taking it for a sweet fruit).
- Neither Valmiki telling has him swallow the sun. Kishkindha: pushed back by its heat after 300 yojanas. Uttara: reaches the sun's chariot unharmed, then goes after Rahu and Airavata.
- Treat the Chalisa with full respect: a devotional hymn; we only say Valmiki tells it differently.
