2.57.1
  प्रतिबुद्धो मुहुर् तेन शोकोपहतचेतनः ।
  अथ राजा दशरथः स चिन्तामभ्यपद्यत ॥
  pratibuddho muhur tena śokopahatacetanaḥ, atha rājā daśarathaḥ sa cintāmabhyapadyata
  Then King Daśaratha, his consciousness afflicted by sorrow, repeatedly woke up due to that (sorrow) and fell into anxiety.

2.57.2
  रामलक्ष्मणयोश्चैव विवासाद्वासवोपमम् ।
  आविवेशोपसर्गस्तं तमः सूर्यमिवासुरम् ॥
  rāmalakṣmaṇayoścaiva vivāsādvāsavopamam, āviveśopasargastaṃ tamaḥ sūryamivāsuram
  A calamity (upasarga) overcame him, who was like Indra, due to the exile of Rama and Lakshmana, just as darkness (tamas) or a demon overcomes the sun.

2.57.3
  अर्धरात्रे दशरथः संस्मरन्दुष्कृतं कृतम् ।
  कौसल्यां पुत्रशोकार्तामिदं वचनमब्रवीत् ॥
  
  On the sixth night after Rama had gone into the forest, King Dasharatha, at midnight, remembering the evil deed (karma) he had committed, spoke these words to Kausalya, who was tormented by grief for her son.

2.57.4
  यदाचरति कल्याणि शुभं वा यदि वाशुभम् ।
  तदेव लभते भद्रे कर्ता कर्मजमात्मनः ॥
  yadācarati kalyāṇi śubhaṃ vā yadi vāśubham, tadeva labhate bhadre kartā karmajamātmanaḥ
  O auspicious one (kalyāṇī), whatever good or evil deed one performs, the doer obtains that very fruit which is born of their own actions (karma). O blessed one (bhadre).

2.57.5
  गुरु लाघवमर्थानामारम्भे कर्मणां फलम् ।
  दोषं वा यो न जानाति स बाल इति होच्यते ॥
  guru lāghavamarthānāmārambhe karmaṇāṃ phalam, doṣaṃ vā yo na jānāti sa bāla iti hocyate
  He who does not comprehend the gravity or insignificance of situations, the outcome (phalam) of actions at their commencement, or their potential flaws (doṣa), is indeed called a child.

2.57.6
  कश्चिदाम्रवणं छित्त्वा पलाशांश्च निषिञ्चति ।
  पुष्पं दृष्ट्वा फले गृध्नुः स शोचति फलागमे ॥
  kaścidāmravaṇaṃ chittvā palāśāṃśca niṣiñcati, puṣpaṃ dṛṣṭvā phale gṛdhnuḥ sa śocati phalāgame
  Someone cuts down a mango grove and instead waters *palāśa* trees. Greedy for fruit after seeing flowers, he grieves when the fruits appear.

2.57.7
  सो ऽहमाम्रवणं छित्त्वा पलाशांश्च न्यषेचयम् ।
  रामं फलागमे त्यक्त्वा पश्चाच्छोचामि दुर्मतिः ॥
  so'hamāmravaṇaṃ chittvā palāśāṃśca nyaṣecayam, rāmaṃ phalāgame tyaktvā paścācchocāmi durmatiḥ
  I am that foolish person (durmati)! Having cut down a mango grove and watered *palāśa* trees, I now grieve afterward, having abandoned Rāma at the time of fruit-bearing.

2.57.8
  कुमारः शब्दवेधीति मया पापमिदं कृतम् ।
  तदिदं मे ऽनुसंप्राप्तं देवि दुःखं स्वयं कृतम् ॥
  
  O Kausalyā, this sin was committed by me - the young man (kumāra) who was renowned and an archer - thinking: 'that boy is a sound-piercer (śabdavedhin)'. Therefore, O goddess, this suffering, created by myself, has now come to me.

2.57.9
  संमोहादिह बालेन यथा स्याद्भक्षितं विषम् ।
  एवं ममाप्यविज्ञातं शब्दवेध्यमयं फलम् ॥
  saṃmohādiha bālena yathā syādbhakṣitaṃ viṣam, evaṃ mamāpyavijñātaṃ śabdavedhyamayaṃ phalam
  Just as a child might ignorantly eat poison due to delusion (saṃmoha), so too has this consequence, stemming from my sound-piercing (śabdavedha) act, come to pass unbeknownst to me.

2.57.10
  देव्यनूढा त्वमभवो युवराजो भवाम्यहम् ।
  ततः प्रावृडनुप्राप्ता मदकामविवर्धिनी ॥
  devyanūḍhā tvamabhavo yuvarājo bhavāmyaham, tataḥ prāvṛḍanuprāptā madakāmavivardhinī
  O goddess, you were unmarried, and I was to become the crown prince. Subsequently, the rainy season arrived, intensifying infatuation and desire.

2.57.11
  उपास्यहि रसान्भौमांस्तप्त्वा च जगदंशुभिः ।
  परेताचरितां भीमां रविराविशते दिशम् ॥
  upāsyahi rasānbhaumāṃstaptvā ca jagadaṃśubhiḥ, paretācaritāṃ bhīmāṃ ravirāviśate diśam
  Having absorbed the earthly moisture and scorched the world with its rays, the sun then enters the terrifying southern direction, which is frequented by spirits of the dead.

2.57.12
  उष्णमन्तर्दधे सद्यः स्निग्धा ददृशिरे घनाः ।
  ततो जहृषिरे सर्वे भेकसारङ्गबर्हिणः ॥
  uṣṇamantardadhe sadyaḥ snigdhā dadṛśire ghanāḥ, tato jahṛṣire sarve bhekasāraṅgabarhiṇaḥ
  The heat suddenly vanished; smooth, soft clouds became visible. Subsequently, all the frogs, deer, and peacocks rejoiced.

2.57.13
  पतितेनाम्भसा छन्नः पतमानेन चासकृत् ।
  आबभौ मत्तसारङ्गस्तोयराशिरिवाचलः ॥
  patitenāmbhasā channaḥ patamānena cāsakṛt, ābabhau mattasāraṅgastoyarāśirivācalaḥ
  Covered by the fallen water and repeatedly by the continuously falling water, the mountain, with its intoxicated deer, shone like a large body of water (toyarāśi).

2.57.14
  तस्मिन्नतिसुखे काले धनुष्मानिषुमान् रथी ।
  व्यायाम कृतसंकल्पः सरयूमन्वगां नदीम् ॥
  tasminnatisukhe kāle dhanuṣmāniṣumān rathī, vyāyāma kṛtasaṃkalpaḥ sarayūmanvagāṃ nadīm
  During that very pleasant time, I, armed with a bow and arrows, a warrior, and determined for the practice (of hunting), followed the Sarayu river.

2.57.15
  निपाने महिषं रात्रौ गजं वाभ्यागतं नदीम् ।
  अन्यं वा श्वापदं कं चिज्जिघांसुरजितेन्द्रियः ॥
  nipāne mahiṣaṃ rātrau gajaṃ vābhyāgataṃ nadīm, anyaṃ vā śvāpadaṃ kaṃ cijjighāṃsurajitendriyaḥ
  With unrestrained senses, desiring to kill a buffalo or an elephant that had come to the river at night, or any other wild animal.

2.57.16
  अथान्धकारे त्वश्रौषं जले कुम्भस्य पर्यतः ।
  अचक्षुर्विषये घोषं वारणस्येव नर्दतः ॥
  athāndhakāre tvaśrauṣaṃ jale kumbhasya paryataḥ, acakṣurviṣaye ghoṣaṃ vāraṇasyeva nardataḥ
  Then, in the darkness, beyond the range of my sight, I heard a sound in the water, like that of a pitcher being filled, or as if of a trumpeting elephant.

2.57.17
  ततो ऽहं शरमुद्धृत्य दीप्तमाशीविषोपमम् ।
  अमुञ्चं निशितं बाणमहमाशीविषोपमम् ॥
  tato'haṃ śaramuddhṛtya dīptamāśīviṣopamam, amuñcaṃ niśitaṃ bāṇamahamāśīviṣopamam
  Then I, having drawn out a blazing arrow that resembled a venomous snake, released a keen arrow, as I myself was like a venomous snake.

2.57.18
  हा हेति पततस्तोये वागभूत्तत्र मानुषी ।
  कथमस्मद्विधे शस्त्रं निपतेत्तु तपस्विनि ॥
  
  At dawn, a clear voice arose from a forest-dweller who was falling into the water, crying 'Alas! Alas!' The voice was human, saying: 'How could a weapon strike a hermit (tapasvin) like me?'

2.57.19
  प्रविविक्तां नदीं रात्राव् उदाहारो ऽहमागतः ।
  इषुणाभिहतः केन कस्य वा किं कृतं मया ॥
  praviviktāṃ nadīṃ rātrāv udāhāro'hamāgataḥ, iṣuṇābhihataḥ kena kasya vā kiṃ kṛtaṃ mayā
  I had come at night to a secluded river for water. By what arrow have I been struck? What wrong have I done, or to whom?

2.57.20
  ऋषेर्हि न्यस्त दण्डस्य वने वन्येन जीवतः ।
  कथं नु शस्त्रेण वधो मद्विधस्य विधीयते ॥
  ṛṣerhi nyasta daṇḍasya vane vanyena jīvataḥ, kathaṃ nu śastreṇa vadho madvidhasya vidhīyate
  Indeed, how can the killing of someone like me, a sage who has renounced violence (nyasta daṇḍa) and lives in the forest on forest produce, be perpetrated by a weapon?

2.57.21
  जटाभारधरस्यैव वल्कलाजिनवाससः ।
  को वधेन ममार्थी स्यात् किं वास्यापकृतं मया ॥
  jaṭābhāradharasyaiva valkalājinavāsasaḥ, ko vadhena mamārthī syāt kiṃ vāsyāpakṛtaṃ mayā
  Who would indeed seek the death of me, one who wears matted hair and garments of bark and deerskin? Or what harm have I done to him?

2.57.22
  एवं निष्फलमारब्धं केवलानर्थसंहितम् ।
  न कश्चित् साधु मन्येत यथैव गुरुतल्पगम् ॥
  evaṃ niṣphalamārabdhaṃ kevalānarthasaṃhitam, na kaścit sādhu manyeta yathaiva gurutalpagam
  No one would consider such an undertaking, which is begun fruitlessly and leads only to misfortune, as proper, just as no one would consider proper the sin of one who defiles the guru's bed.

2.57.23
  नेमं तथानुशोचामि जीवितक्षयमात्मनः ।
  मातरं पितरं चोभावनुशोचामि मद्विधे ॥
  nemaṃ tathānuśocāmi jīvitakṣayamātmanaḥ, mātaraṃ pitaraṃ cobhāvanuśocāmi madvidhe
  I do not grieve so much for this loss of my own life (ātman). Instead, I lament for both my mother and father in my present condition.

2.57.24
  तदेतान्मिथुनं वृद्धं चिरकालभृतं मया ।
  मयि पञ्चत्वमापन्ने कां वृत्तिं वर्तयिष्यति ॥
  tadetānmithunaṃ vṛddhaṃ cirakālabhṛtaṃ mayā, mayi pañcatvamāpanne kāṃ vṛttiṃ vartayiṣyati
  Therefore, how will this aged couple, whom I have supported for so long, sustain themselves when I have met my death (pañcatva)?

2.57.25
  वृद्धौ च मातापितरावहं चैकेषुणा हतः ।
  केन स्म निहताः सर्वे सुबालेनाकृतात्मना ॥
  vṛddhau ca mātāpitarāvahaṃ caikeṣuṇā hataḥ, kena sma nihatāḥ sarve subālenākṛtātmanā
  Both my aged parents and I have been struck down by a single arrow. By whom, indeed, were we all killed? By a mere child, an undeveloped soul (ātman)!

2.57.26
  तं गिरं करुणां श्रुत्वा मम धर्मानुकाङ्क्षिणः ।
  कराभ्यां सशरं चापं व्यथितस्यापतद्भुवि ॥
  taṃ giraṃ karuṇāṃ śrutvā mama dharmānukāṅkṣiṇaḥ, karābhyāṃ saśaraṃ cāpaṃ vyathitasyāpatadbhuvi
  Upon hearing that pitiable cry, the bow with its arrow fell to the ground from my hands, as I, distressed and intent on upholding my natural law (dharma), stood.

2.57.27
  तं देशमहमागम्य दीनसत्त्वः सुदुर्मनाः ।
  अपश्यमिषुणा तीरे सरय्वास्तापसं हतम् ॥
  taṃ deśamahamāgamya dīnasattvaḥ sudurmanāḥ, apaśyamiṣuṇā tīre sarayvāstāpasaṃ hatam
  Having reached that spot, I, with a sorrowful spirit and a very dejected mind, saw the ascetic lying dead, killed by an arrow, on the bank of the Sarayū river.

2.57.28
  स मामुद्वीक्ष्य नेत्राभ्यां त्रस्तमस्वस्थचेतसं ।
  इत्युवाच वचः क्रूरं दिधक्षन्निव तेजसा ॥
  sa māmudvīkṣya netrābhyāṃ trastamasvasthacetasaṃ, ityuvāca vacaḥ krūraṃ didhakṣanniva tejasā
  He, having looked at me with his eyes - me, who was terrified and uneasy in mind - spoke these cruel words, as if about to burn me with his intense spiritual power (tejas).

2.57.29
  किं तवापकृतं राजन् वने निवसता मया ।
  जिहीर्षुरम्भो गुर्वर्थं यदहं ताडितस्त्वया ॥
  kiṃ tavāpakṛtaṃ rājan vane nivasatā mayā, jihīrṣurambho gurvarthaṃ yadahaṃ tāḍitastvayā
  O King, what wrong have I, who was dwelling in the forest, committed against you that I was struck by you while intending to fetch water for my guru (teacher)?

2.57.30
  एकेन खलु बाणेन मर्मण्यभिहते मयि ।
  द्वावन्धौ निहतौ वृद्धौ माता जनयिता च मे ॥
  ekena khalu bāṇena marmaṇyabhihate mayi, dvāvandhau nihatau vṛddhau mātā janayitā ca me
  Indeed, with one arrow, as I was struck in a vital spot, two blind and aged individuals - my mother and my father - have been killed.

2.57.31
  तौ नूनं दुर्बलावन्धौ मत्प्रतीक्षौ पिपासितौ ।
  चिरमाशाकृतां तृष्णां कष्टां संधारयिष्यतः ॥
  tau nūnaṃ durbalāvandhau matpratīkṣau pipāsitau, ciramāśākṛtāṃ tṛṣṇāṃ kaṣṭāṃ saṃdhārayiṣyataḥ
  Surely, those two weak, blind, and thirsty individuals, waiting for me, will endure for a long time this difficult thirst that hope has created.

2.57.32
  न नूनं तपसो वास्ति फलयोगः श्रुतस्य वा ।
  पिता यन्मां न जानाति शयानं पतितं भुवि ॥
  na nūnaṃ tapaso vāsti phalayogaḥ śrutasya vā, pitā yanmāṃ na jānāti śayānaṃ patitaṃ bhuvi
  Surely, there is no fruit/benefit (yoga) from my penance (tapas) or sacred learning, since my father does not recognize me as I lie fallen on the ground.

2.57.33
  जानन्नपि च किं कुर्यादशक्तिरपरिक्रमः ।
  भिद्यमानमिवाशक्तस्त्रातुमन्यो नगो नगम् ॥
  jānannapi ca kiṃ kuryādaśaktiraparikramaḥ, bhidyamānamivāśaktastrātumanyo nago nagam
  And even if he knew, what could he do? He is in a state of powerlessness (śakti) and without resources, like one mountain unable to save another crumbling mountain.

2.57.34
  पितुस्त्वमेव मे गत्वा शीघ्रमाचक्ष्व राघव ।
  न त्वामनुदहेत् क्रुद्धो वनं वह्निरिवैधितः ॥
  pitustvameva me gatvā śīghramācakṣva rāghava, na tvāmanudahet kruddho vanaṃ vahnirivaidhitaḥ
  O Rāghava, you yourself go quickly and inform my father. May he not, enraged, consume you (with a curse) just as a blazing fire consumes a forest.

2.57.35
  इयमेकपदी राजन्यतो मे पितुराश्रमः ।
  तं प्रसादय गत्वा त्वं न त्वां स कुपितः शपेत् ॥
  iyamekapadī rājanyato me piturāśramaḥ, taṃ prasādaya gatvā tvaṃ na tvāṃ sa kupitaḥ śapet
  O King, this is the sole path leading to my father's hermitage (āśrama). Go and appease him, lest he, in his anger, curse you.

2.57.36
  विशल्यं कुरु मां राजन्मर्म मे निशितः शरः ।
  रुणद्धि मृदु सोत्सेधं तीरमम्बुरयो यथा ॥
  viśalyaṃ kuru māṃ rājanmarma me niśitaḥ śaraḥ, ruṇaddhi mṛdu sotsedhaṃ tīramamburayo yathā
  O King, free me from this arrow; a sharp arrow is in my vital spot (marma). It obstructs (my vital functions), just as a powerful water current erodes a soft, elevated bank.

2.57.37
  न द्विजातिरहं राजन्मा भूत्ते मनसो व्यथा ।
  शूद्रायामस्मि वैश्येन जातो जनपदाधिप ॥
  na dvijātirahaṃ rājanmā bhūtte manaso vyathā, śūdrāyāmasmi vaiśyena jāto janapadādhipa
  O King, I am not a twice-born (dvijāti). Do not let your mind (manas) be distressed. O ruler of the country, I was born to a Śūdra woman by a Vaiśya.

2.57.38
  इतीव वदतः कृच्छ्राद्बाणाभिहतमर्मणः ।
  तस्य त्वानम्यमानस्य तं बाणमहमुद्धरम् ॥
  itīva vadataḥ kṛcchrādbāṇābhihatamarmaṇaḥ, tasya tvānamyamānasya taṃ bāṇamahamuddharam
  As he spoke thus with difficulty, his vital parts struck by an arrow, I pulled out that arrow while he was being bent down.

2.57.39
  जलार्द्रगात्रं तु विलप्य कृच्छान्मर्मव्रणं संततमुच्छसन्तम् ।
  ततः सरय्वां तमहं शयानं समीक्ष्य भद्रे सुभृशं विषण्णः ॥
  jalārdragātraṃ tu vilapya kṛcchānmarmavraṇaṃ saṃtatamucchasantam, tataḥ sarayvāṃ tamahaṃ śayānaṃ samīkṣya bhadre subhṛśaṃ viṣaṇṇaḥ
  O venerable lady, then, seeing him (the hermit's son) lying on the Sarayu, his body wet with water, lamenting with difficulty, and constantly sighing from his vital wound, I became exceedingly distressed.
